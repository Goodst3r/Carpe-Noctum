CHANGES
1. Threshold and source registry (build list item 1)
   - Test -> Limits lists every limit, target and formula the field tools
     use, filterable by label: Spec, Expert, Company, Unknown.
   - Each entry keeps its source, revision, page or section, units,
     conditions, plant and checked date. Tapping a tag opens a source sheet.
   - Company values are read where they already live: the job's project
     profile, the 42/54 meter-setup TX reference (the 204/258 workflow has
     none), or the built-in local preset (31 dBmV at 54 MHz, 43 dBmV at
     860 MHz, separate 38 dBmV return TX). A blank value shows "Unknown -
     not entered"; an entered 0 stays 0. The 750 MHz example profile's
     32/43 dBmV launch levels are described as labeled examples.
   - Two existing approximations are labeled Unknown: the 0.1 %/F cable-loss
     temperature correction (traced only to an indexed copy of CommScope
     MX0398) and the 0.004 per C resistance default (no recorded source).
   - Not yet registered: example targets on older screens (Test ->
     References workbook and sample targets, plant-picker start levels).
   - No new storage. Opening the registry never changes the job.

2. Field calculators (build list item 4), Test -> Calculators
   - dB converter: dBmV, dBm, dBuV, mV, mW at 75 or 50 ohm.
   - Ripple to distance: 491.79 x VP / ripple spacing (published as 492).
   - Pre-EQ tap to distance: spacing between two mismatches (not the
     distance from the modem or node). DOCSIS 2.0/3.0 SC-QAM upstream,
     24 taps, 1.28 / 2.56 / 5.12 Msym/s, +/- half-tap range. Not OFDMA.
   - OFDM power: restate power per 6, 24 or 1.6 MHz and over the occupied
     band. Assumes flat power; a tilt needs SCTE 270 section 30.14.
   - Echo delay: round trip 2 x length / (VP x c).
   - VP from the four catalog cables that list one, or typed in (0.50-1.00).
   - Invalid or missing input shows a reason, never a number.

3. Tilt / EQ / pad solver (build list item 2), Test -> Calculators ->
   Pad / EQ balance
   - Readings and targets at two pilots give the level error at each pilot
     and the tilt error, with the direction to move the input EQ and pad.
   - GainMaker 1 GHz (HGD, GaN HGD, HGBT, LGD 65/86, LE manual/thermal/AGC):
     picks the input EQ for tilt, then the input pad for level at the high
     pilot, from Cisco OL-31156-01 / OL-31155-01 and S-A 716336 Rev D values
     (pads 0-20.5 dB in 0.5 dB steps; 1 GHz cable EQs 1.5-30 dB with their
     52-1000 MHz loss chart). Predicts the levels after the change.
   - Never offers a value outside the sourced list; no interpolation across
     the chart's 86-550 MHz gap; the uncharted 0 dB jumper, inverse EQs and
     870 MHz EQs are left out and listed on the card.
   - Advice is worded from the pick (keep or change each part, with the
     high-pilot level predicted after the EQ change) and says whether the
     result lands inside the GainMaker procedure's +/-0.5 dB window.
   - Pilots must sit inside the model's forward band (no 54 MHz pilot on a
     65/86 amplifier). The chart's top column is headed 1000 MHz in the
     guide and 1002 MHz in data sheet 716336; both are accepted.
   - Quantum18 HGD and HGBT align electronically in AOI's QuantumLink Local
     app (SA guide V02102025): they get that note, not part values. The LE
     data sheet only describes electronic control and no public LE
     procedure or pad/EQ list was found: it gets a note too. The BA uses
     plug-in gain-adjust pads (0 dB fitted; 2, 4 and 6 dB in the lid, from a
     preliminary data sheet) and gets pad direction in those steps.
   - Checked against a LibreOffice-recalculated spreadsheet: three synthetic
     cases per GainMaker family (balanced, high tilt, low level).

6. DOCSIS health card (build list item 5), Test -> DOCSIS health
   - Compares a reading you already have with the cable modem requirements
     in the CableLabs DOCSIS physical-layer specifications and with your
     own company window. Nothing is read from a modem, CMTS or meter, and
     nothing is saved in the job.
   - Downstream SC-QAM: -15 to +15 dBmV per 6 MHz channel (PHYv3.0 C01
     Table 6-22). Downstream OFDM: -15 to +15 dBmV per 6 MHz and -9 to +21
     dBmV per 24 MHz as printed (PHYv3.1 I20 Table 45); other occupied
     bandwidths move the 24 MHz row by 10 log10(B/24) and are labeled
     Expert. Total input power: below 40 dBmV for a 3.1 modem, below 33 dBmV
     for a 3.0-only modem. Upstream: headroom between the modem's total
     transmit power and Pmax, 65 dBmV by default (PHYv3.1 I20 7.4.12.2).
   - Spec ranges are capability limits, not targets; each result is stated
     in words (inside, below, above, margin in dB), never color alone. The
     modem generation (total input) , how the OFDM power was measured and how
     the modem is working (upstream) must be chosen: there are no silent
     defaults. The 65 dBmV Pmax applies only to a 3.1 modem on a 3.1 CMTS.
   - Company windows (lowest and highest level) start unknown, blank is
     never zero, and they are saved on this device only under the new
     browser-storage key nightsweep-docsis-windows-v1. They are not part of
     a job, its full backup or the job file format; one set serves every job
     and both plants. Existing storage keys and the job format are unchanged.
   - Ten new registry rows (six spec or expert, four company) appear in
     Test -> Limits. Sources and what was re-read:
     research/build3/DOCSIS_HEALTH_SOURCES.md.
   - Practice: the custom-curves exercise gains a step (eleven in total)
     and the feature index gains entry 40.
   - Layout: the Test sub-tab row now wraps when its tabs do not fit (a
     ninth tab overflowed the page at 768 px).

7. Leakage tools (build list item 7), Test -> Leakage
   - FCC leakage limit check against 47 CFR 76.605(c) Table 1 (analog 15 and
     digital 13.1 uV/m at 30 m at 54 MHz and below and over 216 MHz; analog
     20 and digital 17.4 uV/m at 3 m over 54 up to 216 MHz), restating a
     reading at another distance by the inverse-distance relation (Expert,
     approximate).
   - Distance to 3 m, a CLI estimator (47 CFR 76.611(a)(1): 10 log10 of
     (1/theta) x sum of E^2 over leaks of 50 uV/m or more, compared with
     64; no frequency correlation), a detector check (directly connected
     source or calibrated leak; company tolerance starts unknown) and a
     personal leak log with the 76.1706 fields and a CSV export.
   - A notebook and calculator, not the company's official leak record and
     not a filing. The log, saved detector checks and the two company
     settings (repair threshold, detector tolerance) are kept on this
     device only under the new browser-storage key nightsweep-leakage-v1,
     outside jobs and their backups. Existing storage keys and the job
     format are unchanged. The screen asks for a pole, tap or cross street,
     never customer names, addresses or account numbers.
   - Twelve registry rows (seven spec, one spec secondary for 47 CFR 76.614,
     two expert flagged secondary, two company). Sources and what was read:
     research/build3/LEAKAGE_SOURCES.md.
   - A leak counts in the CLI only when it is at least 50 uV/m measured 3 m
     from the leak with the test frequency in 108-137 MHz (76.611(a)(1),
     (b)(1)); any other frequency, or none, is shown as needing correlation
     or a frequency. The card does no correlation.
   - Leaks in the CLI card are separated by spaces, new lines or semicolons;
     a comma with no space (1,500 or 120,5) is refused. The fraction
     examined is 0.001 to 1, not both fraction and strand miles.
   - A restated reading gets a neutral mark, not a green check; an estimate
     from under 75% of the plant gets none either. The near-field edge
     (wavelength / 2 pi) is warned about at the entered frequency.
   - If this browser will not store a change it is kept and written with the
     next change that saves; unreadable stored data is copied to
     nightsweep-leakage-v1-unreadable before it is replaced; data from a
     newer version is never overwritten. A saved detector check keeps the
     tolerance in force when it was saved. Editing one company setting never
     changes the other. Mark repaired can be undone with Reopen.
   - Practice: the custom-curves exercise gains a step (twelve in total)
     and the feature index gains entry 41.

9. Outage first-response card (build list item 8), Job -> Outage
   - Prompts for the first steps of an outage call: twenty-four items in
     eight sections (confirm the scope, safety on arrival, power, node and
     optics, actives in order, tell the right people, if damage or theft is
     suspected, record the times). It opens with "Follow your company's
     outage procedure first" and says it is not any company's procedure. A
     supervisor has not reviewed it against the local outage procedure, and
     that review is needed before coworkers use it (research/build3/
     OUTAGE_SOURCES.md lists the questions).
   - The wording is original; no NCTI or SCTE text is copied. The list has
     no numbers, limits or levels, so it adds no registry row. It points at
     SCTE's 2026 Critical Infrastructure Protection Subcommittee only to say
     its evidence-collection standard (still being written) is not
     implemented here.
   - No customer identifiers: the notes are a plant place (node leg, amp or
     pole), four times, what was found and done, who was told as a role,
     and three optional damage or theft notes. No field for a name,
     address, phone number or account number, and no photos or locations. A
     test fails if a field label asks for an identity.
   - Same engine, controls and storage rules as the safety card, under its
     own browser-storage key nightsweep-outage-v1, outside jobs and
     backups. Start a new call clears the ticks and every note; Save as text
     prepares a short note to check before sharing. New Job sub-tab: Outage.
   - Practice: the custom-curves exercise gains a step (fourteen in total)
     and the feature index gains entry 43.

10. Impairment decision tree and signature cards (build list item 11), Test -> Impairments
   - Pick what the meter or spectrum shows (ten pictures) and read the
     matching card: what it looks like, what usually causes it, what it is
     easily mistaken for, and five steps (analyze, localize, prioritize,
     repair, validate). Cards: standing wave, suckout, resonant peaking,
     roll-off, tilt, excessive flat loss, ingress, cellular (LTE) ingress,
     common path distortion, impulse and burst noise.
   - Looks and causes follow SCTE 280 2022 (paraphrased, cited by section);
     the five-step order is the workflow of SCTE's April 2026 PNM series;
     the steps under each card are general practice labeled Expert. The
     tilt and flat-loss cards say the source text was not read. It is the
     technician's own aid, not a company procedure. Sources and what was
     read: research/build3/IMPAIRMENT_SOURCES.md.
   - The standing-wave card gives the distance between two mismatches
     (492 x VP / ripple spacing, the Calculators formula) and lists the
     pairs of points on the saved route that far apart, within a search
     width (10 percent, changeable for one search, a registered Expert
     value). A candidate is a place to look first, not a finding.
   - Nothing is read from a meter and nothing is stored: no new storage key,
     no change to the job format (tested).
   - Practice: the custom-curves exercise gains a step (fifteen in total)
     and the feature index gains entry 44.

INDEPENDENT REVIEW OF THE LEAKAGE TOOLS
Three reviewers (sources, math and storage, UI and product rules), each
finding checked by a skeptic, confirmed 35 findings in the first draft; all
are fixed. Among them: the far-field statement credited to SCTE 293-12 was
read in SCTE 221; 30 m was credited to 47 CFR 76.609(h); 47 CFR 76.614 was
missing and "many operators repair sooner" had no source; "counts in the
CLI" ignored the test frequency; a failed write lost later entries; unreadable
or newer stored data was overwritten; comma-separated numbers could be read as
two leaks; and several test names claimed more than they checked.

INDEPENDENT REVIEW
Four reviewers (math, provenance, UI, regressions), each checked by a
skeptic, confirmed five defects in the first commit; all are fixed: a
204/258 TX reference row the app forbids, example profile values called
entered, the missing pre-EQ spacing caveat, a stale Limits list after a
job change, and the sheet closing on a padding tap. Calculator outputs are
now bounded, "-0.00" is shown as 0.00, and calculators and the registry
are left out of the printed report.
