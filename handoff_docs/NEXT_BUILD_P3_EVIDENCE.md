ATX/Cisco identities remain distinct; published orderable equals signs are preserved. The ATX 42 zero row's printed `GAGM-EQREV-204M-0` is retained as an anomaly, never invented as `42M-0`; no Cisco 204 zero alias is invented.

`lookupLoss(partId, frequencyMHz)` returns known/estimated/unknown with value, units, source and reason. It strictly rejects blank/nonfinite/negative/invalid-decimal and out-of-domain frequency input; no extrapolation. Published table points mean typical insertion loss, not measured plant response or a tolerance. Application estimates are piecewise-linear in MHz only across adjacent published points with ratio ≤ 1.5. At 11 MHz, standard 42 order 6 is 3.96 dB estimated; frequencies strictly between 5/10 remain unknown. Standard 204 has numeric loss only at 5/204 MHz, and every interior is unknown. The 1 GHz 86–550 and 1.2 GHz 105–204/258–550 gaps remain unknown. Each known endpoint and the complete part list stays visible when another output is unavailable.

All 592 numeric loss cells match the separately parsed raw-source fixture; 28 source-dash cells remain unknown. An initial broader zero-row representation was caught by source QA, reproduced by a new RED assertion, then corrected. The numeric source tables never changed. Standard 42's 24 published B tilt cells and A's 24 endpoints/12 nominal labels are also checked. F1–F3/R1–R3, source-specific 1000/1002 MHz, zero jumpers, wrong families/splits/slots/models, AOI electronic controls, and monotonic/identity/sign invariants are covered at 1e-9 dB numeric tolerance; scalar display is 0.1 dB, source tables retain published precision. The 1.2 GHz 1.5 row keeps two decimals; 28.5/30 keep 1.5 at 1218 MHz.

`Alignment` consumes a stable shared adapter whose 20 prior labels and complete point arrays match the pre-P3 fixture exactly.
