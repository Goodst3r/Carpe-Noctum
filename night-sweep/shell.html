Power calculation, cable resistance and limits

Equipment load: use a matching manufacturer current-versus-voltage curve when available. Input watts use I = W ÷ (V × PF); VA uses I = VA ÷ V. Known current holds the entered amperage constant. Watts with PF = 1 is a visible unity-PF estimate.

Shared cable: downstream currents add on each upstream section. Voltage drop uses section current × series loop resistance; cable/contact loss uses I² × R. The solver recalculates equipment current as voltage changes.

Resistance: published center-conductor plus shield values are referenced to 68°F / 20°C. Built-in values use your power cable temperature and coefficient. Custom loop resistance is used as entered at its actual temperature. Extra contact resistance is also used as entered. Parallel returns and waveform differences are not modeled.

Missing data: unknown cable or equipment data leaves results unavailable. A source curve is never extrapolated outside its published voltage range. A failed voltage solution is reported instead of displaying a partial total.

Equipment, gain and loss references

Match the full model, housing and installed configuration. An upgrade faceplate is a different part from the original it replaces.

Choose the right tool

Understand the result

Prediction: a calculated level based on your inputs. Measurement: a reading you entered or a supported CSV value. They are kept separate.

Dash (—): missing or unsupported data, not a zero reading. Check the frequency range, cable, chosen feed and gain values.

Gain shortfall: the entered gain is below the gain required at one or both markers. Reserve: the remaining margin after restoring your target. Positive margin alone does not establish output capability.

Saved on this device: your working copy is local. Export a project backup to keep it outside the app; include every named job you need.

How the calculation works

At a tap: tap-port level = incoming level − preceding cable loss − tap loss. The next trunk section uses through loss, not tap-port loss.

Active equipment: choose Active amplifier in the route equipment type. Add measured or configured gain at two frequencies, or a frequency/gain table. Output = input + operating gain − additional output loss. Gain interpolation stops outside the supplied frequency range and hardware band.

Distance: span length plus slack follows the selected upstream path. Branch distances stay separate. TDR distances are already one-way; do not divide a meter distance by two.

Branches: select the actual upstream splitter/coupler port in “Fed from.” Deleting a parent leaves a warning; the route is never silently reconnected.

Normalized sweep: output minus source, then zeroed at the reference frequency: −[path loss(f) − path loss(reference)]. Launch tilt is removed. Use absolute mode for expected meter levels.

Estimates: cable loss uses published dB/100 ft points interpolated in √frequency and a 0.1%/°F correction from 68°F. Passive point tables interpolate linearly in MHz; band tables use their stated band, with the upper band chosen at shared boundaries.

Limits: no passive/cable extrapolation outside published data. Maximum-point interpolation is not a guaranteed maximum between points. Planning mode falls back to maximum values where typical values are not published and labels that basis.

Field additions: plugin EQs, pads, drop cable, connectors and other losses must be added explicitly using cable-only/custom rows. The model does not predict reflections, suck-outs, ingress, MER, compression or active alignment. An amplifier uses your installed operating-gain points; published gain remains a labeled configuration reference.

Nodes and ONX-630 evidence

Job → Details → Node equipment: choose the installed EN9000 or GS7K model, split, module and operating bandwidth. Manufacturer hardware limits do not establish available spectrum. Choose the exact optical/RPD configuration in Power; EN9000 needs an installed or measured load.

Job → ONX files: select Sweep, Ingress or OneCheck, the stage and asset, then upload saved meter files available on your phone. Files stay offline on this device. Supported VIAVI TestGroup and CDM2 JSON, gzip and a ZIP containing one JSON trace can show explicit measurement samples. Ambiguous or unsupported files remain originals with a reason. PDF, XML, HTML and images stay attached.

Readable CSV: use explicit frequency_mhz and level_dbmv columns; use response_db for relative sweep values. OneCheck channel tables use channel, frequency_mhz, level_dbmv, mer_db. No test-point correction is applied. Open Job → ONX files, then use Grading aid to compare verified originals against your blank-by-default company profile; calculated grades never establish physical acceptance.

Backup: Files → Back up project includes the original attachments. Open project replaces the working job. Export before uninstalling or clearing storage. A trace filename typed into a measurement record does not itself attach a file.

Limits: 8 MiB per attachment, 12 MiB of originals and 30 attachments per job. Project exports are limited to 20 MiB. Real ONX exports and physical Android behavior still need field testing.
